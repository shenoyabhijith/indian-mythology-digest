"When a person casts off all desires dwelling in the mind and is satisfied in the Self alone, they are said to be of steady wisdom."
The Story

What Happened

Arjuna asks Krishna a powerful question: what does a sage look like? How do they speak, sit, walk? Krishna's answer describes not an external renunciate, but an internal state — someone who has uprooted attachment from the mind itself, not just from the world outside.
Why It Matters

The Insight

The Sthitaprajna isn't a monk on a mountain — it's a state accessible to anyone. Krishna describes someone who moves through the world, happy and active, yet untouched by praise or blame. This is the Gita's vision of psychological freedom: full engagement without emotional slavery.
