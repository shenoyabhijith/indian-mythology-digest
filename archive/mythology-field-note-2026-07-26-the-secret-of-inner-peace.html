The Verse

Bhagavad Gita, Chapter 2 — Sthitaprajna

प्रजहाति यदा कामान् सर्वान् पार्थ मनोगतान् |
आत्मन्येवात्मना तुष्टः स्थितप्रज्ञस्तदोच्यते ||
prajahāti yadā kāmān sarvān pārtha manogatān |
ātmanyevātmanā tuṣṭaḥ sthitaprajñastadocyate ||
"When a person casts off all desires dwelling in the mind and is satisfied in the Self alone, they are said to be of steady wisdom."
The Story

What Happened

Arjuna asked Krishna one of the most personal questions anyone can ask: 'What does a wise person look like? How do they speak? How do they sit? How do they walk?' He wasn't asking about a monk on a mountain — he was asking if it was possible to be wise while living in the world. Krishna's answer describes a person who has not renounced the world, but has renounced their slavery to it. This person moves through life fully engaged — they work, they love, they laugh, they weep — but inside they are untouched. Praise does not inflate them; blame does not crush them. Desires come and go like clouds, but their inner sky remains clear. They have found a source of happiness within themselves, so they no longer need the world to make them happy.
Why It Matters

The Insight

The Sthitaprajna is not a superhuman ideal — it is a description of what happens when you stop outsourcing your happiness. Most of us are on a treadmill: if this happens, I'll be happy. If I get this, I'll be peaceful. Krishna says: the peace was always inside you. You just covered it with wanting. The steady-minded person has simply stopped covering it. They still participate fully in life — they just don't need life to give them anything. This is the deepest freedom: to be happy without conditions.
Today's Lesson

How to Apply This

Try this today: do something kind for someone without wanting recognition. Help a colleague, compliment a stranger, give without expecting anything back. Notice how it feels to act from inner fullness rather than outer need. That feeling is a glimpse of the steady mind.
Reflect On This

A Question for You

What are you waiting for to be happy? The job? The relationship? The money? What if the happiness you're waiting for is already here, hidden beneath the waiting itself?
When a person is satisfied in the Self alone, unmoved by the comings and goings of desire — that is the state of steady wisdom.
